Find the tips of a TOFD defect automatically

A Python pipeline denoises your B-scan, detects the upper and lower diffraction echoes, then checks every result against the raw signal.

The data: data_1

The example B-scan is a picture of 96 columns and 176 rows. Each column is one A-scan (one probe position), each row one depth sample.

The program turns grey levels into amplitudes: mid-grey (127.5) means zero, black is −1 and white is +1. Three zones stand out:

  • Around row 25: the lateral (surface) wave, nearly identical on every column.
  • Rows 70 to 90: the two diffraction echoes of the defect. Strong on the left columns, fading away towards the right.
  • Rows 140 to 160: the back-wall echo, so strong that it saturates at ±1.

1 · Cluster

Not all A-scans look alike: on data_1 the echoes of the defect are clear on the left and almost gone on the right. The first step sorts the A-scans into groups of similar signals.

What the program does

  • Each A-scan is centred (its median is subtracted) and divided by its largest absolute value, so every signal lies between −1 and 1. Groups then depend on the shape of the signal, not on its strength.
  • A k-means (scikit-learn, 10 restarts, fixed seed) assigns every A-scan to one of n_clusters groups. The main script uses 3.
  • The offset and scale of each A-scan are kept, to restore the original amplitudes after denoising.

Why

A single network trained on all the A-scans would learn an average signal and blur the echoes that exist in only some of them. With one group per kind of signal, each network has a simple, repetitive shape to learn.

Main setting

n_clusters: too few and different signals get mixed; too many and some groups have only a handful of A-scans to learn from.

2 · Denoise

Noise is random, echoes are not: the same echo shape appears in every A-scan of a group. An autoencoder is a small network that learns exactly that.

What the program does

  • One autoencoder per cluster: a linear layer with a ReLU compresses each A-scan into 20 values, a second linear layer rebuilds the full A-scan from them.
  • Training on mini-batches of 5 random A-scans of the cluster, 2000 iterations by default (1500 in the main script), with the Adam optimiser: the learning rate starts at 0.001 and shrinks by 0.1 % at every iteration.
  • The loss is 90 % reconstruction error and 10 % penalty on the size of the weights, which keeps the network simple and stops it from copying the noise.
  • The weights kept at the end are a running average (decay 0.99) of those seen during training, more stable than the last step alone.
  • The output is rescaled with the stored offsets and scales. The raw signal is kept untouched, for step 5.

Why

Squeezing the A-scan through only 20 values forces the network to keep what the group has in common (the echoes) and to lose what is random (the noise).

Limit

The network can also smooth or slightly shift an echo. That is why the result is checked against the raw signal in step 5.

Main settings

n_hidden (20), n_iterations, batch_size (5).

3 · Envelope

A raw echo oscillates between positive and negative lobes, so its maximum is not a good arrival time. The Hilbert envelope gives one smooth bump per echo, and the top of the bump is the arrival time.

What the program does

  • Surface and back-wall waves first: the mean amplitude at each depth is computed over all A-scans and smoothed; every stretch above 20 % of the maximum is marked. The first stretch is the surface wave, the last one the back-wall wave. On data_1: samples 24 to 33 and 139 to 162.
  • The search window is what lies between them: samples 34 to 138 here.
  • Unless you choose one, the program picks the best A-scan: among the 15 most energetic ones, the one with the most reliable peaks.
  • The envelope is the absolute value of the analytic signal (Hilbert transform). Peaks are searched inside the window; they must stand out by at least 10 % of the highest value and be at least 3 samples apart.
  • On A-scan 1 of data_1, the two tips are found at samples 74 and 86.

Why

Restricting the search to the window prevents the huge surface and back-wall echoes from being mistaken for the defect.

Main setting

min_peak_prominence: how much a bump must stand out to count as an echo.

4 · Separate

When the two tips are close, their bumps overlap and the envelope shows a single lobe: a plain peak search finds only one tip.

What the program does

  • It takes the top of the lobe (amplitude A) and draws a band between 0.85 A and A.
  • It walks left and right until the curve leaves the band: this delimits the zone of the first peak.
  • Outside this zone, it looks for a second true local maximum, no further than the half-height width of the lobe (50 samples at most), and at least 30 % as high as A.
  • If one is found, it becomes the second tip, flagged as less reliable. If not, nothing is invented: one tip is reported with the warning “tips merged”.
  • The same distance and height rules reject a second peak that is only noise or another echo, even when the peak search had found two.

Why

Two simple rules avoid false doublets: a second tip must be close to the first and high enough. Earlier attempts (second derivative, band towards the bottom) invented a second tip on isolated peaks and were abandoned.

Main settings

SeparationConfig: max_separation_factor (1.0), min_relative_second_peak_amplitude (0.3), band_ratio (0.15), max_absolute_separation (50).

5 · Verify

The network could have shifted or smoothed an echo. The last step checks the result against the raw B-scan, without the network.

What the program does

  • For each depth of the search window, it averages the Hilbert envelope of the raw signal over all A-scans. The two tips show up as two intense horizontal bands of the B-scan.
  • Around each of the two strongest plausible peaks, it builds a packet of 5 to 10 rows, growing while the intensity stays above 50 % of the peak. If the packets touch, they are cut at the valley between the peaks.
  • The position of a tip is the centre of mass of its packet.
  • Each tip of the pipeline is compared with its packet: within 5 samples the result is declared correct; otherwise it is replaced by the packet centre and the height is recomputed.
  • If the raw B-scan shows a single lobe, it cannot judge each tip separately: nothing is corrected, and a warning is printed when a tip lies outside the lobe.

Why

Averaging over all A-scans makes the check independent of the network, and much less sensitive to noise than a single A-scan.

From tips to height

With the sound velocity c and half the probe spacing d, the depth of a tip with arrival time t is √((t·c/2)² − d²). The height of the defect is the difference between the depths of the two tips.

Main settings

tolerance_samples (5), min_packet_rows (5), max_packet_rows (10).

How TOFD works

Two ultrasonic probes are placed on either side of a weld: one transmits, the other receives.

Both tips of a defect diffract the wave and each sends back an echo, between the lateral wave and the back-wall echo. Their arrival time gives the depth of each tip, hence the height of the defect.

What the tool does not claim to do

When the two tips are very close, their echoes merge in the envelope. The tool flags it (“tips merged”) instead of inventing a second peak, and the fallback detection requires a visual check.

Idea under consideration: sparse deconvolution (matching pursuit) to separate overlapping echoes.

B-scan denoising

Step 2 of the pipeline, on its own: remove the noise without erasing the echoes you want to measure.

🧠

One autoencoder per group

Each network is trained on mini-batches of 5 A-scans and learns to rebuild the clean echo, not the noise.

The denoised result is only used to find the tips; the final position is compared with the raw A-scan.
