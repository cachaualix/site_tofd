5 steps, from noisy signal to reliable tip

Each step fixes a weakness of the previous one.

1

Cluster

A k-means groups similar A-scans together.

2

Denoise

One autoencoder per group, trained on mini-batches of 5 A-scans.

3

Envelope

The Hilbert envelope reveals the peaks that stand out enough.

4

Separate

A second peak is searched near the main one, with realistic safeguards.

5

Verify

Each tip is compared with the raw signal, and replaced if the gap is too large.

How TOFD works

Two ultrasonic probes are placed on either side of a weld: one transmits, the other receives.

Both tips of a defect diffract the wave and each sends back an echo, between the lateral wave and the back-wall echo. Their arrival time gives the depth of each tip, hence the height of the defect.

What the tool does not claim to do

When the two tips are very close, their echoes merge in the envelope. The tool flags it (“tips merged”) instead of inventing a second peak, and the fallback detection requires a visual check.

Idea under consideration: sparse deconvolution (matching pursuit) to separate overlapping echoes.
